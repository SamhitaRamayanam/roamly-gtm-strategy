# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard & Strategic Bet, Module 2 (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Who is Roamly Groups actually for, based on everything you now know?:** Roamly Groups is for the Reluctant Milestone Organizer—the Maid of Honor, Best Man, or milestone birthday planner who holds the sole curation and booking authority for an event of 6 to 10 adults.

They are not looking for a democratic committee tool or a collaborative social feed. They have already picked the destination, set the dates, and narrowed down the experience. They need a mechanism that gives them enforcement leverage: locking in high-stakes, private inventory (like private boat charters or winery buyouts) while delegating payment collection and hard deadlines directly to the platform.
- **What is the one thing that makes it worth choosing over the competitor?:** Guaranteed private hold with zero personal credit liability and zero-login guest checkout.

Against Airbnb Experiences, Viator, and the status quo DIY stack, Roamly is the only platform where the organizer can freeze peak inventory on a vetted expert's calendar for 24 hours without floating thousands of dollars on their personal card, while participants can pay their exact share in under 15 seconds via mobile web with Apple/Google Pay—without downloading an app or registering an account.
- **What is the biggest threat to that position, based on your battlecard?:** Airbnb’s Upstream Lodging Monopoly and Low-Barrier Feature Parity.

Because Airbnb owns the accommodation anchor (the 8-person villa or cabin), they capture group intent, party size, and travel dates weeks before the planner searches for daytime activities.

If Airbnb introduces a simple "Split with your house guests" hold toggle for experiences inside their post-booking lodging confirmation flow, Roamly’s functional software wedge (hold + split) disappears. Roamly cannot compete on lodging inventory or catalog scale; its defense depends entirely on maintaining strict, premium host vetting (protecting organizers from low-quality duds) and partnering directly with non-Airbnb luxury vacation rentals and boutique concierges to intercept milestone planners before Airbnb cross-sells them.
- **Of all the signals you’re seeing, which one changes how you think about the market? Hint: looking at how your primary competitor wins, what does that tell you about what Roamly Groups needs to do differently to take that buyer?:** The critical signal that fundamentally upends the initial assumption is Airbnb’s post-booking conversion rate driven by their upstream lodging monopoly.

Initially, the strategy treated Roamly Groups as a direct competitor in the "search and discovery" phase of activity planning. But looking at how Airbnb actually wins reveals an uncomfortable truth: Airbnb doesn't win on product superiority, curation, or payment mechanics—they win on chronology.

By securing the 8-person villa or cabin first, Airbnb intercepts the organizer at zero Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) and captures all the downstream intent (dates, headcounts, location) before the organizer even considers opening another tab.

What This Tells Us Roamly Groups Must Do Differently
If Airbnb’s dominance is structural (chronology) rather than functional (features), Roamly cannot win simply by offering a better split-payment checkout inside our own search engine. We must fundamentally alter how and where the buyer encounters the product:

Stop Waiting for Activity Search; Intercept the Lodging Confirmation:

Roamly cannot wait for an organizer to search for "things to do in Scottsdale." We must meet the buyer at the exact moment lodging is locked. This means forging distribution integrations with non-Airbnb group lodging players—vacation rental managers (AvantStay, Sonder, VRBO hosts, luxury villa concierges). When an 8-guest house is booked, the confirmation receipt or digital concierge guide should feature: "Lock in your group's private headline experience with Roamly — hold now, split payments across your guests."

Shift from "Activity Catalog" to "The Un-fuck-upable Anchor Event":

Airbnb wins casual, fill-in-the-gap activities ($30 walking tours, drop-in pasta making) because they are already in the app. Roamly should not compete for daytime filler. Instead, position Roamly Groups around the single headline event of the trip—the high-stakes private catamaran, the vineyard buyout, the chef's table. Milestone organizers will gladly leave Airbnb's ecosystem for the trip's centerpiece event because the cost of a dud is socially catastrophic.

Make the Product Work as an External Utility:

Because we do not own the initial traveler identity the way Airbnb does, our product must have negative friction. Airbnb forces every guest into their walled garden (app downloads, accounts, verifications). Roamly’s wedge must be an unbranded, open utility: a universal payment link that opens in any mobile browser, lets guests tap Apple Pay or Google Pay in 10 seconds, and requires zero logins.
- **Complete the sentence: “I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when because”:** I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when we stop trying to be a top-of-funnel activity search engine and instead position ourselves as the post-lodging coordination utility for the trip’s single ‘cannot-fail’ private event, because Airbnb controls the lodging anchor that dictates travel chronology, but their open-catalog model and single-card checkout completely break down when a milestone organizer needs guaranteed host quality and zero-liability split payments for their group’s headline celebration.
- **“I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by”, your strategic commitment, not what you’re building but how you’ll win with it.:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by establishing itself as the default, frictionless execution bridge between the lodging booking and the group’s centerpiece celebration—securing upstream distribution with independent vacation rentals and empowering the trip leader to lock in high-stakes private experiences without personal credit risk or forced account creation, thereby turning the organizer’s need for social enforcement into our primary conversion and acquisition engine.

How we win, not what we build:
Owning the Timing, Not the Discovery: We bypass the impossible SEO/PPC fight against Airbnb’s home screen by intercepting group planners at the post-lodging trigger point through boutique property managers and rental confirmations.

Exploiting the Incumbent's Blind Spot: Airbnb cannot easily offer frictionless, zero-login guest checkouts without undermining their own registered-user walled garden. We win by treating our checkout as an open, 15-second utility.

Monopolizing the Anchor Event: We leave the $35 walking tours to the mass aggregators and intentionally position our inventory around the single, private, high-AOV event the crew cannot afford to have ruined.

Leveraging Social Dynamics for Distribution: We design around compliance rather than consensus, turning the organizer into our operational partner who distributes the link and enforces the 24-hour clock on our behalf.
- **“To make this bet, I am deprioritizing”, every strategic bet means giving something up. Naming the sacrifice is the proof a real decision was made.:** To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:

Shared Itinerary & Collaborative Planning Features: Giving up democratic group planning tools (voting widgets, group chat, shared drag-and-drop canvases) to build strictly for the single organizer who wants compliance, speed, and enforcement over debate.

Catalog Volume & Casual/Solo Filler Experiences: Walking away from competing for every hour on a traveler’s calendar ($30 public walking tours, low-cost museum audio walks) to focus host onboarding and inventory merchandising exclusively on high-stakes, high-AOV private buyouts.

Mandatory Participant Account Capture: Sacrificing immediate, forced user sign-ups and app downloads for invited attendees, accepting that we won’t capture full profile data on day one in exchange for zero checkout friction and maximum 24-hour payment conversion.

General Top-of-Funnel B2C Discovery & Broad Paid Search: Abandoning high-cost, generic B2C travel search marketing (PPC bidding on "things to do in X") to channel our marketing budget into upstream B2B2C distribution partnerships with vacation rental managers and boutique lodging platforms.
