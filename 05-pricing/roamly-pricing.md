# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5

## Pricing Goal
- **Goal:** Enter a new market (penetration / share)
- **Why:** Our strategic bet establishes that Airbnb dominates travel chronology by securing the lodging transaction first, but fails at group checkout because forced single-card payments and mandatory app logins create friction. Our wedge is becoming the default coordination engine for high-stakes, private centerpiece celebrations. To prove this category exists and capture market share from Airbnb's downstream flow, we must prioritize zero friction to adoption over short-term software monetization. Charging upfront fees or subscriptions would discourage organizers from using the tool, driving them back to messy Venmo requests. We win by driving checkout volume, proving liquidity to hosts, and establishing dominance as the group booking utility.

## Pricing Model
- **Model:** Hybrid (combination)
- **Who pays & for what:** The Lead Organizer (The Buyer) pays $0 upfront. They experience value through risk mitigation, relief from debt collection, and calendar reservation. Adding organizer-side software fees destroys the top of the funnel.

The Participants (The Users/Payers) pay an embedded 3%–5% split-coordination convenience fee added directly to their individualized mobile-web checkout link (e.g., $150 ticket + $6 convenience fee = $156 total). Value for participants is frictionless, transparent guest checkout via Apple/Google Pay without forced app downloads.

The Host Community pays a 15% marketplace take-rate deducted from the gross booking value upon completed checkout. Value for hosts is calendar protection, zero group flake risk, and guaranteed high-margin private buyouts.

## Packaging Approach
- **Structure:** Combination
- **Audiences & fit:** We serve three audiences (Organizers, Participants, and Hosts). One static catalog cannot serve them all because casual solo/duo travelers have vastly different operational needs than a 10-person private milestone buyout. Packaging separates standard individual bookings from dedicated group buyouts, ensuring the organizer is automatically guided into a structured hold-and-split flow without gating core coordination tools like link sharing or guest checkout.
- **Tiers:** Tier 1: Standard Individual / Public Bookings
Target: Solo travelers & couples ($35–$60 AOV).

Features: Instant checkout, single-card payment, public open tours.

Economics: 15% host take-rate; $0 traveler fee.

Tier 2: Roamly Groups Private Buyout ⭐️ (Target Tier: Route 80%+ of volume)
Target: Milestone organizers (6–12 guests) booking headline anchor events ($1,000–$3,500 total booking value).

Features: 24-hour calendar hold lock, zero-login mobile web checkout (Apple/Google Pay), automated SMS/WhatsApp payment ledger ("4 of 8 paid"), community-upvoted itinerary badges.

Economics: 15% host commission + 4% participant-side convenience split fee; $0 organizer platform fee.

Tier 3: Concierge Buyouts (High-Touch / Partner-Sourced)
Target: Luxury villa rentals & high-net-worth groups ($4,000+ booking value) intercepted upstream via property management software (e.g., AvantStay).

Features: 48-hour holds, multi-vendor custom bundling (e.g., private chef + catamaran), dedicated offline concierge support.
Economics: 18% host take-rate + $99 flat concierge booking fee.

## Research to Validate
- **Method:** Conjoint Analysis
- **Assumption to validate:** We need to validate whether passing a 3%–5% coordination convenience fee to invited participants at the mobile-web checkout screen causes cart abandonment compared to baking the entire fee into the host retail price or charging an upfront flat fee to the organizer.
- **Biggest risk if wrong:** The one thing that would change everything if you got it wrong:

Participant fee sensitivity at checkout. If invited friends see a fractional $6 convenience surcharge and refuse to complete their payment, the 24-hour hold will expire unpaid. This would result in high slot-release rates, alienating our 12,000 vetted hosts by locking up their prime weekend inventory without converting. If this proves true, we must immediately pivot to an all-inclusive pricing model where the take-rate is completely absorbed by the host margin and hidden within the ticket price.

### What I changed after AI review
Eliminated Organizer-Side Software Fees:
The initial draft suggested charging organizers a $15–$25 group setup fee. I removed this entirely because it contradicted our primary strategic bet: the organizer is already taking on the social burden of organizing; penalizing them with an upfront fee before their friends even agree to pay re-introduces the friction that causes cart abandonment.

Shifted the Fee to the Participant's Per-Seat Checkout Drawer:
I adjusted the convenience fee to be distributed proportionally to each guest ($5–$8 on a luxury private experience). When paying via Apple Pay on mobile web, an incremental convenience charge is far less noticeable than a lump-sum charge to a single buyer.

Established a Clear Fail-Safe Contingency:
I tightened the Conjoint Analysis mandate to focus on cart abandonment thresholds. If testing reveals that guests drop off when seeing the split fee, our clear operational fallback is to absorb the fee entirely into the host's advertised private ticket price.
